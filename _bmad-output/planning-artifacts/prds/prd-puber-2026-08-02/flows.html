Ride state machine

Six states on the happy path and three terminals. Every terminal resolves the payment, and a delivered trip is pursued until it resolves too — there is no way for a ride to end and leave money in limbo.

in flight delivered terminal, no trip system-initiated recovery

Each state answers exactly one question. REQUESTED means awaiting authorisation — nothing else, entered once, never returned to. WAITING_MATCH means funds are held and the ride is in the pool; it is the only state matching reads, so an unfunded ride is invisible rather than merely rejected. OFFERED means a driver is deciding. MATCHED means a driver has accepted and is on the way.

Every recovery path returns to WAITING_MATCH — never to REQUESTED, because the hold already exists and must not be taken twice. A declined or expired offer and a driver who vanishes before pickup both land there. Auto-completion reuses COMPLETED; how a ride was completed is recorded alongside it rather than as a state of its own, so no report has to match two values to find completed rides.

Payments, and when money actually moves

Two phases: the fare is authorised as a hold when the ride is requested, and captured only when a trip is delivered. A ride that delivers no trip releases the hold; a capture is then pursued until it settles, never until a retry budget runs out.

hold in place money collected terminal, nothing captured

Two terminals look alike and are not. FAILED is an authorisation declined before any trip happened — nobody was charged and nothing was lost. CAPTURE_FAILED is a delivered trip whose money is gone. They stay separate states because they differ in kind, not in provenance: one is a ride that never happened, the other is the only case in the system that is genuine revenue loss — which is why only the second is counted and summed as one FR-46.

Only the provider can declare a hold dead. A capture that fails is retried with jittered backoff, keeps the payment in AUTHORIZED while it retries, and goes on retrying across process restarts — there is no cap, because a valid hold on a delivered trip is money owed and a cap would throw it away. A provider that is unreachable has not answered, and that is the next retry rather than an outcome. CAPTURE_FAILED is reached only when the provider reports the hold expired, was revoked, or was cancelled. So a hold always ends captured, voided, or provably uncapturable — never in limbo.

This is the one place payment state reaches back into rides. Every other coupling in the system runs ride → payment; FR-51 runs the other way, refusing a new ride request while the rider's last payment is unsettled, or for 30 minutes after their most recent CAPTURE_FAILED. It sits at request admission and nowhere else — once a hold is confirmed the ride stops waiting on payment entirely, so nothing a rider is already waiting on pays for this guard.

Refusals are counted by reason, never as one total FR-46. A one-active-ride refusal is ordinary rider behaviour and tells you nothing; a spike in either payment-driven reason is a provider failing or recovering, seen at the one place a rider actually feels it rather than inferred from the provider's own telemetry. Collapsed into a single counter, that signal disappears.

The rider's session

From opening the app to reading history. Every step is a capability the backend exposes — there is no screen, only the endpoint the screen would call.

  1. 01

    Check for a ride already in flight FR-4

    Looked up by rider identity, not ride id — losing the id is not a dead end. Returns at most one.

  2. 02

    Quote a trip FR-1

    Fare, distance and ETA without creating anything. Indicative only — the price is re-derived at request. With nobody online it still returns fare and distance, just no ETA.

  3. 03

    Request the ride FR-2 FR-3 FR-51

    Carries a payment-method token, since no account stores one. Fare locks here. Three separate refusals guard this door, each distinguishable from the others: a ride already in flight — every non-terminal state counts as active; a previous payment that has not settled yet; and a CAPTURE_FAILED less than 30 minutes old. The last two clear themselves — one when the payment settles, the other on a timer — with nothing marked against the rider.

  4. 04

    The fare is authorised before anyone is dispatched FR-9

    Held in the background — the ride is persisted as REQUESTED the instant the rider commits and the id comes straight back; the hold follows, moving it to WAITING_MATCH. A declined card ends the ride at PAYMENT_FAILED without a driver ever being offered it.

  5. 05

    Watch the driver approach FR-5 FR-6

    Once a driver has accepted: who is coming, where they are now, and an ETA that moves as they do. This is the line in the vision made literal.

  6. 06

    Cancel, any time before the trip starts FR-16

    Releases any assigned driver, withdraws any outstanding offer, voids the hold. A mismatched identity is refused without revealing the ride exists.

  7. 07

    See what was charged FR-7

    Final fare and payment outcome — captured, refunded, or left uncaptured because the hold was no longer capturable — so the payment machine has a visible consequence. A capture still retrying is not yet an outcome; the rider sees one once the payment settles.

  8. 08

    Read history FR-8

    Most recent first, bounded by a result limit.

The driver's session

A driver owns their own availability and can only ever act on the ride assigned to them.

  1. 01

    Read the whole working state in one call FR-21

    Declared status, whether the system can currently hear them, any offer awaiting an answer with its pickup, dropoff, fare and distance, plus the active ride if one is running.

  2. 02

    Go online FR-20

    An explicit tap — the system never puts a driver online for them. Going offline depends on the ride, not on being engaged: with an offer outstanding it is released and they go offline; once accepted, it is refused until they finish.

  3. 03

    Report position on a heartbeat FR-26

    Feeds matching, the rider's live view, and — mirrored to the columnar store — the distance and density analytics. Silence never changes their declared status FR-29.

  4. 04

    Receive an offer, and everything else that matters FR-45

    The push channel carries offers plus the events a driver must react to: the rider cancelled, the offer was withdrawn, the ride was auto-completed.

  5. 05

    Accept — or decline FR-22 FR-23

    Declining returns the ride to the pool immediately instead of making the rider wait out the timeout — and the same driver will not be offered that ride again.

  6. 06

    Start the trip, then complete it FR-24

    Each valid only from the preceding state and only on their own ride. Completing returns them to AVAILABLE.

  7. 07

    Review completed rides FR-25

    The driver-side counterpart to the rider's history.

  8. 08

    The shift ends — by choice, or eventually by absence FR-30

    Going offline is theirs to do. But an idle driver unreachable far longer than any staleness window is set offline by the system, so someone who ended Monday by switching their phone off is not still working on Wednesday.

Where the system deliberately does nothing. A ride waiting on authorisation has no timeout at all. Timing it out would free the rider to request again and place a second hold while the first is outstanding — during a provider outage that manufactures a hold storm against the one dependency the system cannot control. Instead the one-active-ride rule becomes natural admission control: a rider with a stuck ride cannot generate more. FR-51 closes the sibling gap — a rider whose ride has gone terminal while its payment has not settled cannot generate more either, which is what keeps the uncapped capture retry from turning one struggling provider into a pile of unpaid trips.

The principle behind this, the stranded-hold row, and the uncapped capture retry: bound the windows where the system can determine an outcome; never bound a wait for external truth it will eventually receive. "We looked and found nobody" is an answer the system holds. "We do not know yet" is not, and inventing one there is what causes the damage — which is exactly what a capture retry cap did before it was removed: it turned a provider that had not yet answered into a write-off.

Settled by the architecture pass

Every mechanism question this document used to list as open has been answered. Nothing product-level is outstanding.

QuestionAnswer
How payments are excluded from the stress test Swap the provider, keep the path — the payment service and its state machine still run; only the outbound call is replaced. Skipping the path would stress-test code that does not exist in production
Transport for the rider's live driver position Polling. The position only changes when a heartbeat lands, so pushing more often carries no new information
The three staleness windows Sized as one ordered set with every other time constant. The ordering is what must survive retuning: idle < matched < in-progress < session expiry
How long matching waits before giving up Fixed in the same set, at roughly six offer attempts' worth of time
The hold-leak invariant Asserted in tests and alerted on, never swept automatically. The one real race — cancelling while authorisation is in flight — is handled by a flag on the payment
Whether a ride awaiting authorisation should time out No. See the recovery callout above

What is still open sits below this altitude: outbox retry and backoff windows, batch sizes, pool and replica counts, partition counts. The sizing method is settled — arrival rate × service time, drain must exceed arrival with headroom — and the stress test exists to produce the numbers.
